Lesson 1: Diagnosing Bias vs Variance

~12 min · Mission: minimizing error in MDN3R3D

Every other lesson in this track depends on this one. Before you can decide whether to add capacity, add data, or add regularization to your model, you need to know which problem you actually have. Guessing wastes runs; this lesson gives you a two-number test.

The idea

Your model's error has two independent failure modes:

  • Bias — the model is too simple (or undertrained) to fit even the data it has seen.
  • Variance — the model fit the training data too specifically, and doesn't generalize to new points.

You can't see either one directly. What you can see is two numbers, both already computed by your notebook every time it trains: train_loss and val_loss (from train_result["history"], plotted by save_training_plots). Comparing them tells you which failure mode you're in.

train_lossval_lossDiagnosis
high≈ train_lossHigh bias (underfitting)
low>> train_lossHigh variance (overfitting)
low≈ train_lossGood fit

This table — and the fixes for each row — are captured in full in the Bias/Variance Cheat Sheet. Keep that open in a tab; you'll come back to it every time you tune a hyperparameter.

Primary source: Andrew Ng, Improving Deep Neural Networks, Week 1 — the "Basic Recipe for Machine Learning" video lays out exactly this diagnostic flow. Watch it once; this lesson is the applied version for your own model.

Check yourself

Three scenarios. Classify each one before checking the answer — guessing after seeing the answer defeats the point.

1. train_loss = 0.85, val_loss = 0.87, both plateaued early and high. What's going on?
2. train_loss = 0.05, val_loss = 0.61, and the gap keeps widening after epoch 40.
3. train_loss = 0.09, val_loss = 0.11, both still slowly falling together at the last epoch.

One that isn't just table lookup

4. In MDN3R3D, lambda_fk is currently 0.1. If you set it to 5.0 and train_loss on the NLL term stays stubbornly high while fk_loss drops to near zero, what's the most likely cause?

Question 4 isn't in the diagnostic table on purpose — it's testing whether you can reason about your own composite loss, not just memorize a row.
